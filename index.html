How the prediction works

1. Cycle lengths. A cycle is the number of days from one period start to the next. Gaps under 15 or over 60 days are ignored, since they usually mean a missed log.

2. Outliers. With four or more cycles, lengths far from your median (a stressful month, illness, travel) are left out of the average. They show as dashed bars in History.

3. Recent cycles count more. The last 12 valid cycles are averaged with weights that fade by 15% per cycle back, so the estimate follows your current pattern.

4. Few cycles. With fewer than three cycles the average is pulled toward 28 days, and the window stays wide until more data arrives.

5. Window. The margin is your weighted standard deviation, at least one day, and it grows for cycles further ahead.

6. Ovulation. Estimated at 14 days before the predicted start. The fertile window is the 5 days before ovulation plus the day after.

This is a statistical estimate from your own history. It is not contraception and not medical advice.
